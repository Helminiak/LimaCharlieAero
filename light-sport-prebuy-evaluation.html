Prebuy boundary

A prebuy is structured risk control before money changes hands. It is not a promise that the aircraft will never have problems, and it is not a return-to-service statement.

Three Prebuy Levels

Choose the Review Depth That Fits the Aircraft and Decision.

A simple listing review and a deep records audit are different services. The level should match the aircraft value, record quality, travel risk, seller cooperation, and buyer tolerance for uncertainty.

Level 1

Courtesy Glance

Fast triage for obvious concerns before spending travel money.

  • Listing, photos, N-number basics when available, and obvious logbook/photo red flags
  • Questions to ask the seller before the buyer commits more time
  • Best for: “Should I keep looking at this aircraft?”

Boundary: Not an inspection, no return-to-service implication, no hidden-condition assurance, and no physical aircraft access unless separately arranged.

Level 2

Standard Prebuy Evaluation

Structured decision support for a serious buyer before closing.

  • Airframe, engine, propeller, avionics, records, equipment list, and operating limitations review
  • S-LSA / E-LSA / E-AB status implications and ROTAX records, SB/SL/SI, rubber age, oil/fuel history, gearbox/propeller/vibration context where applicable
  • Visible-system condition, installed configuration, open squawks, and buyer decision report

Boundary: Not a guarantee of future condition. Scope depends on access, seller cooperation, records quality, weather, location, and certificate privileges.

Level 3

Forensic Records and Condition Audit

Deep review for expensive aircraft, messy records, suspected damage history, or maximum risk reduction.

  • Deeper logbook chronology, damage / repair / alteration trace, and component identity review
  • Compliance context for manuals, bulletins, safety directives, operating limitations, and manufacturer correspondence
  • Discrepancy matrix, engine lifecycle review, serial numbers, missing records, and escrow / negotiation support summary

Boundary: More expensive and slower, requires records access and cooperation, and still is not future-condition assurance.
